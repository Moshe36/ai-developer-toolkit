# Writing Clearly and Concisely

Based on William Strunk Jr.'s *The Elements of Style* (1918) — the most enduring guide to plain English.

## When to Use This Skill

Use whenever you write prose that a human will read:

- README files, documentation, technical explanations
- Commit messages, PR descriptions
- Error messages, UI copy, help text
- Code comments that explain *why*
- Any summary or report

**If you're writing sentences for a human to read, apply these rules.**

## The Rules That Matter Most

These are the ones violated most often — internalize these first:

**10. Use the active voice.**
Active is more direct and vigorous than passive.
- Weak: *The tests were run by the CI pipeline.*
- Strong: *The CI pipeline ran the tests.*

**11. Put statements in positive form.**
Make definite assertions. Avoid tame, hedging, non-committal language.
- Weak: *He did not think the approach was very useful.*
- Strong: *He thought the approach useless.*

**12. Use definite, specific, concrete language.**
Prefer the specific to the general, the concrete to the abstract.
- Weak: *A period of unfavorable weather set in.*
- Strong: *It rained every day for a week.*

**13. Omit needless words.**
Every word must earn its place. Cut without mercy.
- *the question as to whether* → *whether*
- *owing to the fact that* → *because*
- *he is a man who* → *he*
- *at this point in time* → *now*

**16. Keep related words together.**
The position of words shows their relationship. Don't make the reader search for the connection.

**18. Place emphatic words at the end.**
The end of a sentence is the position of greatest emphasis. Put what matters most there.

## Full Rules Reference

### Elementary Rules of Usage
1. Form possessive singular by adding 's (Charles's, not Charles')
2. Use a comma after each term in a series except the last
3. Enclose parenthetic expressions between commas
4. Place a comma before a conjunction introducing a co-ordinate clause
5. Do not join independent clauses with a comma — use a semicolon
6. Do not break sentences in two with a period (no sentence fragments unless emphatic)
7. A participial phrase at the beginning must refer to the grammatical subject

### Elementary Principles of Composition
8. One paragraph per topic
9. Begin each paragraph with a topic sentence
10. Use the active voice
11. Put statements in positive form
12. Use definite, specific, concrete language
13. Omit needless words
14. Avoid a succession of loose sentences
15. Express co-ordinate ideas in similar form (parallel construction)
16. Keep related words together
17. In summaries, keep to one tense
18. Place emphatic words at the end of the sentence

## Quick Self-Check Before Finishing

1. Is every sentence in the active voice unless there's a specific reason for passive?
2. Are there any hedging phrases that could be cut or made definite?
3. Are there any abstract words where a concrete one would be clearer?
4. Is every word earning its place — or is there filler?
5. Does the most important word or phrase land at the end of its sentence?
